Skellig Michael by Car — the Drive Is the Easy Part; the Boat, the Weather and 600 Steps Are Not

Two jagged rocks rise straight out of the Atlantic off the Kerry coast, and most people pull over whether they meant to or not. Our correspondent plots the road to the harbour, the crossing, and the climb — so cousins can decide with their eyes open.

By Shruti, Travel Correspondent

Ring of Kerry — The first time the Skelligs appear on the horizon, most people pull over whether they meant to or not. But the mistake most cousins make, our correspondent writes, is thinking the drive is the hard part. It is not. The hard part is the boat, the weather, the 600 steps, and the decision about whether a few hours on a freezing rock is how you want to spend a day of your holiday.

There is no bridge and no car ferry — the only way onto Skellig Michael is a licensed passenger boat from Portmagee, on the north side of Valentia Island, or Ballinskelligs a few kilometres south. The run out on the R566 from Cahersiveen, down the long hill where the islands suddenly fill the windscreen, is one of the best approach roads in Ireland — and almost nobody talks about it.

The crossing is 45 minutes to an hour of open Atlantic, not a harbour cruise. Boats are licensed by the Office of Public Works, only a fixed number may land each day, and operators cancel when the swell makes landing genuinely dangerous — so the guide counsels booking early and building a buffer day into the itinerary.

Then comes the landing: roughly 600 uneven stone steps cut into the rock, no handrail, no café, no toilets, no shop. Good boots are non-negotiable; you carry in everything you need and carry out everything you bring. The reward, at the summit, is a monastery built by monks who clearly did not believe in doing anything the easy way.
