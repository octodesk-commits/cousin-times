From the Travel Desk — My Irish Cousin

The Burren by Car: The National Park, Aillwee Cave and the Coast Road

The Burren is the one place in Ireland where a sign saying National Park leads you to a crossroads with no car park, no toilets and no gate, because there isn't one. Two hundred and fifty square kilometres of north Clare are given over to bare grey limestone, and the State owns about one per cent of it — the rest is farmed. That single fact decides how you visit: you drive it, you stop where there is somewhere to leave the car, and you walk only where walking is allowed. A pavement of flat slabs — clints — divided by deep fissures called grikes, with Arctic-alpine and Mediterranean plants growing within a few metres of each other because the rock holds heat and the cattle keep the scrub down.
