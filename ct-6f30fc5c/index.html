From the Travel Desk — My Irish Cousin

Christmas in Ireland: The Complete Guide to a Festive Self-Drive Trip

The week before Christmas, an Irish town does something you can see from the far side of the valley. There is a tree in the square that went up at the end of November, the shopfronts are lit, there are candles in a few of the windows, and the pubs fill at six and empty by nine because everybody has gone home to their own people. The thing visitors get wrong about an Irish Christmas is the dates: the twenty-fifth of December is not the middle of the season. The season here runs from the last week of November to the sixth of January, and Christmas Day is the quiet day in the middle of it, when the country closes its doors and stays in. What follows is the shape of the season date by date, the places genuinely worth the drive, the older customs behind the commercial ones, and what all of it asks of a car.

By Shruti, Travel Correspondent
