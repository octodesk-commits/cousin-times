From the Travel Desk — My Irish Cousin

Ireland in February: Short Days, Empty Roads, Early Spring

The first week of February is when an Irish winter stops pretending it is going anywhere and starts showing what comes next. The hedges are still bare, the fields still wet, frost on the windscreen most mornings — but the light has turned. Snowdrops are out under the trees in the old demesnes, the lowland ewes have started lambing, and there is a first thin green along the verges that was not there in January. February carries a poor reputation and it does not deserve it: it is the second coldest month on the 1991–2020 averages at 5.5 degrees, and it is also the month the country climbs out of winter — daylight in Dublin grows by an hour and forty-seven minutes inside the month, and average sunshine jumps from January's 51 hours to 71. Which half of it you book matters more than the month itself.

By Shruti, Travel Correspondent
